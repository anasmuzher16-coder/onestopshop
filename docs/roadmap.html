From mockup to a working store

Nine steps take the approved One Stop Shop mockup to a real store that takes payment, prices shipping live, and lets the shop run its own stock. Five of the nine are built or partly built. What is left is mostly waiting on answers, not on building.

Who is holding what, right now

Us

  • Pick which payment company to ask first
  • Write the approval request
  • Connect a mail service so order emails send

The shop

  • Which till it uses at the counter
  • Canada Post business account number
  • Who holds the website login
  • Product list, photos, box sizes

Payment company

  • A written yes to taking the shop, before checkout is built

Canada Post

  • Terms for shipping cologne by ground, given by phone
  • Access to its new connection for stores

The nine steps

0

Answers before code

Waiting on other people

Nothing has come back yet. These five answers are the only thing between the build and a store that takes money.

  • Ask a payment company, in writing, whether it will take the shop.
  • Confirm the shop's Canada Post small business account and sign up for Canada Post's new connection.
  • Phone Canada Post for the cologne terms.
  • Find out who holds the website login and which till the shop uses.
Done whenOne payment company has said yes in writing.

Waiting on: the payment company, Canada Post and the shop.

1

Foundation

Done

Live at onestopshop-store.anas-dev.workers.dev beside the untouched preview. Still to add: a separate test copy.

  • A new site beside the preview, with a test copy and a live copy.
  • Products move from a hand-written file into a database.
  • Every product gets its own real web address, so Google can find it.

  • A one-day smoke test on a real iPhone, with Low Power Mode on and off.

2

The shop's admin

First version done

Today, Stock and Orders work with sample data. Sign-in is a passcode with a lockout. Still to add: the shop's real products and photos, and the emailed one-time code.

  • Add, edit and remove products from a phone, photo included.
  • Stock counts with a low-stock list and a record of every change.
  • Orders in one list.
  • A home screen with a handful of numbers: sales, orders, average sale, best sellers, slow movers.
Done whenThe owner adds a product from his own phone with nobody helping.

Needs from the shop: the product list, prices, photos, and one of each kind of product weighed and measured in its box.

3

Checkout

Built, no payment yet

Orders place for real, the store prices them itself, tax follows the province, emails are written, privacy and terms pages are drafted. Still to add: card payment, and connecting a mail service so the emails send.

  • The store, not the shopper's phone, works out the final price.
  • Card payment on the payment company's own secure form.
  • Tax by the province the parcel goes to.
  • Emails for order placed, shipped, ready for pickup, refunded, and low stock.
  • No forced account. Shoppers check out as guests.
  • Shipping, returns, privacy and terms pages.
Done whenTest orders succeed in every tax zone, plus one refund and one declined card.
4

Canada Post live

Not started

Shipping still prices from the saved table. Needs the shop's Canada Post account.

  • The shipping price comes from Canada Post at checkout.
  • The saved price table takes over when Canada Post is down, which it is every Saturday 9 pm to Sunday 7 am.
  • Labels bought and printed from the admin, tracking sent to the customer.
  • Pickup orders, pick lists and packing slips that name the box to use.
Done whenThe price quoted to the shopper equals the price on the label, on real test parcels.
5

Cologne and misc

Sections done

Both sections are in the store and the back office, and cologne is held to ground services. Still to add: Canada Post's cologne terms and the shop's real items.

  • Two new sections in the store and in the admin.
  • Cologne listings show size and strength, and carry a ground-only flag.
  • With cologne in the cart, only the ground services are offered.
  • Every misc item gets a shipping type when it is added.
Done whenCanada Post's cologne terms are in hand and the supplier's label paperwork is on file.

If Canada Post says no: cologne launches as pickup only, or ships by Purolator ground.

6

Motion and smoke

Mostly done

A wisp behind the product and on the home page, a puff at the cart, and a still-image fallback. Still to confirm on an iPhone with Low Power Mode on.

  • A thin wisp behind the selected product, lit from behind and tinted to its tile.
  • A short puff when something lands in the cart.
  • The product tile grows into its page in one move.
  • Three levels: live smoke, a slow drift when the phone is saving battery, a still image when motion is turned off.
Done whenIt runs smoothly on an iPhone 16 Plus, Low Power Mode on and off, and never sits over text or price labels.

First 30 days

Done whenThe shop has run a full month without us stepping in.

Decisions that move the dates

  1. Which payment company to ask first. We recommend Stripe, with Square as the second application.
  2. Whose accounts. We recommend the shop owns the Cloudflare, payment and Canada Post accounts, with us added as a member.
  3. Cologne at launch or after. After is safer. It waits on a phone call we do not control.
  4. Which smoke moments ship. Our shortlist is the wisp behind the product and the cart puff.
  5. Till sync and barcode scanning. First version or later. Later is simpler.
