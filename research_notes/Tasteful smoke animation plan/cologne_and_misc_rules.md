# Selling cologne online in Canada and handling a "misc" category: shipping, labelling, regulatory rules and catalogue consequences (as of 2026-11-14)

Conventions: "TEXT" = quoted from the page named. "MEANS" = my reading. COULD NOT VERIFY = no primary source reached, with what would settle it. All pages fetched 2026-11-14.

## 1. Canada Post: are alcohol-based perfumes and colognes mailable within Canada, on which services, with what limits?

### Takeaway
For the general public, alcohol-based perfume is non-mailable. Canada Post does accept it as a TDG "limited quantity" from contract customers and Solutions for Small Business cardholders under an "expanded acceptance program", by ground only (Regular Parcel or Expedited Parcel), never by Xpresspost or Priority, never to air stage (remote fly-in) offices, never outside Canada. Enrolment terms are not published; the shop must call.

### Cited Findings
- TEXT, "Scented items": "Perfumes and toiletries with an alcohol base are considered dangerous goods (flammable liquids). So, they're not allowed in the mail stream. See Non-mailable Matter for more information." - [Canada Post, ABCs of mailing, Examples of how to package and wrap items](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/abcs-of-mailing/examples-of-how-to-package-and-wrap-items.page)
- TEXT, s. 6.1 "Dangerous goods overview" (guide dated "Last Updated: May 29, 2026"): "Dangerous goods, as defined by the Transportation of Dangerous Goods Regulations (TDGR), are non-mailable matter. We won't accept packages that contain dangerous goods or that display dangerous goods symbols, except for lithium batteries described in ABCs of mailing." and "If you're a contract customer or Solutions for Small Business (SfSB) cardholder, an expanded acceptance program is available for: Limited quantities / Excepted quantities / Lithium batteries. For more details, contact your sales representative or call customer service at 1-888-550-6333." - [Canada Post, Non-mailable matter PDF](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/non-mailable-matter.pdf); same text on [Dangerous goods overview page](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/non-mailable-matter/dangerous-goods-overview.page)

### Inferences
- MEANS: the two Canada Post texts are consistent once read together: retail-counter and non-contract mailers cannot send cologne; a contract or SfSB account enrolled in the acceptance program can, as limited quantity, ground only.
- MEANS for the calculator: any cart with a flammable item must (a) offer only Regular Parcel and Expedited Parcel, hiding Xpresspost and Priority; (b) refuse or strip the item for air stage postal codes (load the Canada Post air stage list as a blocklist); (c) keep each box at or under 30 kg gross; (d) trigger a packing note to apply the limited quantity mark and use leak-proof inner packing with absorbent. A mixed cart inherits the most restrictive item's service set.
- MEANS: a 50 to 200 mL bottle is far below the 5 L inner limit, so per-bottle volume is not a practical constraint; the 30 kg box cap is the binding one.
- MEANS: because Canada Post refuses any box "displaying dangerous goods symbols" unless the conditions are met, a marked box tendered on a non-enrolled account is likely to be refused; enrolment must come before the first shipment.

- COULD NOT VERIFY: whether Canada Post allows limited quantity cologne in the same box as non-dangerous goods (hookah parts) or with lithium battery devices. TDG s. 1.17 does not forbid mixed packing with ordinary goods; Canada Post's published text is silent. Ask in the same call.

## 3. TDG Regulations: the limited quantity exemption (s. 1.17) and training

### Takeaway
Cologne is UN1266, Class 3, with a limited quantity index of 5 L. If each inner container is at or under 5 L, the box is at or under 30 kg gross and carries the limited quantity mark, Parts 3 to 8 (documents, safety marks, containment standards, training, emergency plans, reporting) and Part 17 do not apply. No TDG training certificate is required while the exemption conditions are met.

### Cited Findings
- Schedule 1 TEXT (consolidation "current to 2026-10-19 and last amended on 2026-07-01"): "UN1266 PERFUMERY PRODUCTS with flammable solvents and with not more than 20% nitrocellulose, by mass..." Class 3; PG II: special provision 59, column 6(a) "5 L", 6(b) "E2", column 9 "15 L"; PG III: 59, "5 L", "E1", "60 L". - [TDG Regulations full text, Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/FullText.html)
- s. 1.17(1) TEXT: "A quantity of dangerous goods, other than explosives, is a limited quantity if (a) the dangerous goods are in one or more means of containment designed, constructed, filled, closed, secured and maintained so that under normal conditions of transport, including handling, there is no release of the dangerous goods that could endanger public safety; and (b) each outer means of containment has a gross mass that is less than or equal to 30 kg and (i) if the dangerous goods are a solid or a liquid, the goods in each inner means of containment are in a quantity that is less than or equal to the number shown in column 6(a) of Schedule 1". - [TDG s. 1.17](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/page-4.html)
- s. 1.17(2) TEXT: "Parts 3 to 8 and 17 do not apply to the offering for transport, handling or transporting of limited quantities of dangerous goods if each means of containment is legibly and durably marked on one side, other than a side on which it is intended to rest or be stacked during transport, with the mark illustrated in subsection (5)." - same page
- s. 1.17(3) TEXT: inner containers need no mark if "(a) the gross mass of the outer means of containment is less than or equal to 30 kg; (b) the outer means of containment is not intended to be opened during transport; and (c) the outer means of containment is legibly and visibly marked, on a contrasting background, with the mark illustrated in subsection (5)." - same page
- s. 1.17(5) TEXT: "The mark is a square on point, and the line forming the square on point must be at least 2 mm wide. The top and bottom portions must be black and the central portion must be white or a contrasting colour. Each side of the mark must be at least 100 mm long. The letter 'Y' may be displayed in the centre of the mark if the limited quantity is in compliance with the ICAO Technical Instructions. If the size of the means of containment so requires, the length of each side may be reduced to not less than 50 mm, provided that the mark remains clearly visible." Subsections (6) and (7) repealed by SOR/2026-112. - same page
- Excepted quantities s. 1.17.1 table: E2 = 30 mL per inner, 500 mL per outer; E1 = 30 mL per inner, 1 000 mL per outer; needs the excepted quantities mark. - same page
- Training TEXT: "You should always assume you need training. The only time training is NOT required is when you are using an exemption (i.e., special case)." and "As with any exemption, if you cannot meet one of the specified conditions, then the exemption does not apply and you must comply with the entire TDG Regulations. This means you might need a shipping document, a label, a placard, a training certificate and/or a certified container or package". Also: "You must not ship dangerous goods as a 'limited quantity' when the index in Column 6(a) is '0'." - [Transport Canada, Safety awareness materials and FAQ](https://tc.canada.ca/en/dangerous-goods/safety-awareness-materials-faq)
- Class 3 definition, s. 2.18(1) TEXT: liquids are Class 3 "if they (a) have a flash point less than or equal to 60 C using the closed-cup test method"; s. 2.18(2): liquids with flash point above 35 C are excluded if they "do not sustain combustion". - [TDG full text](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/FullText.html)
- Part 17 is "Site Registration Requirements". - same

### Inferences
- MEANS: Part 6 (Training) sits inside "Parts 3 to 8", so a shipper who meets s. 1.17 needs no training certificate; the shipper still carries the duty to classify correctly and pack so nothing leaks.
- MEANS: the old "Limited Quantity"/"Consumer Commodity" word marks referenced on the Transport Canada FAQ and Purolator page are legacy; the current regulation text recognises only the square-on-point mark.
- MEANS: an oil-based attar with no ethanol is only outside Class 3 if its flash point is above 60 C; that is a per-product fact from the supplier's safety data sheet, not something the catalogue can assume from the word "attar".

### Gaps
- Packing group (II vs III) of a given cologne depends on flash point and boiling point; irrelevant to the 5 L index (same for both) but would matter for excepted quantities.
- Did not read special provision 59 in the current consolidation.

## 4. Health Canada: Cosmetic Regulations duties for a fragrance reseller

### Takeaway
The notification duty sits on the manufacturer and the importer, not on a retailer reselling Canadian-sourced stock. But a shop that imports bottles itself (for example direct from a UAE or US wholesaler) is the "importer" and must file a Cosmetic Notification Form within 10 days of first sale, and nobody may sell an un-notified cosmetic. Labels must be bilingual with an INCI ingredient list; fragrance allergen disclosure has applied since April 12, 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Definitions TEXT (consolidation current to 2026-10-19, last amended 2026-04-12): "importer means a person who imports a cosmetic into Canada for the purpose of selling it." "manufacturer ... means ... (a) a person in Canada who sells the cosmetic under the person's own name or under a trademark, design, trade name or other name or mark owned or controlled by the person; or (b) a person in Canada who is authorized to act in Canada on behalf of another person who is not in Canada". s. 2.1: if no one meets "manufacturer", the reference is read as "(a) an importer of the cosmetic". - [Cosmetic Regulations, C.R.C., c. 869](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._869/FullText.html)
- s. 30(1) TEXT: "Every manufacturer and importer shall provide the Minister with the following documents, at the latest 10 days after the manufacturer or importer first sells a cosmetic: (a) a notification on a form obtained from the Minister". - same
- s. 15.3(1) TEXT: "A person must not sell a cosmetic after the 10-day period beginning on the day on which the manufacturer or importer first sells that cosmetic if the Minister has not been provided with the documents required under subsection 30(1)." - same
- Health Canada guide: failure to notify may result in a product being "denied entry into Canada" or removed from sale. The guide does not address retailers or a second importer of an already-notified product. - [Health Canada, Guide for Cosmetic Notifications](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/cosmetics/notification-cosmetics/guide.html)
- s. 18 TEXT: "The information required by these Regulations to be provided on the label of a cosmetic must (a) be shown both in English and in French, except for the INCI name; and (b) be clearly legible". - Cosmetic Regulations, link above
- s. 20 TEXT: "a person must not sell a cosmetic unless its inner label contains (a) a telephone number, email address, website address, postal address or any other information that enables communication with a contact person ... and (b) the identity of the cosmetic". - same
- s. 21.2(1) TEXT: "a list of ingredients must appear on the outer label of a cosmetic, with each ingredient listed only by its INCI name." s. 21.4(3): "the words 'parfum' and 'aroma' ... may be inserted at the end of the list of ingredients". s. 21.5: small packages may put the list on a tag, tape or card, or "on a website, provided that the outer label contains a statement specifying that the list of ingredients appears on that website". - same
- Fragrance allergens, s. 21.4(4) TEXT: "A fragrance allergen must be listed if (a) in the case of a rinse-off product, the concentration ... is greater than 0.01%; or (b) in the case of a leave-on product ... greater than 0.001%." - same
- Dates per Health Canada's Industry Guide: April 12, 2026, new and existing cosmetics must disclose the 24 "List 1" allergens; August 1, 2026, new cosmetics must disclose "List 2"; August 1, 2028, existing products must disclose List 2. The Regulatory Impact Analysis Statement words the same deadlines as July 31, 2026 and July 31, 2028. - [Health Canada, Industry Guide for the labelling of cosmetics](https://www.canada.ca/en/health-canada/services/consumer-product-safety/reports-publications/industry-professionals/labelling-cosmetics.html); [Canada Gazette Part II, SOR/2024-63](https://gazette.gc.ca/rp-pr/p2/2024/2024-04-24/html/sor-dors63-eng.html)
- Industry Guide: identity and net quantity must be in English and French on the principal display panel; "Manufacturers and importers are responsible for making sure their cosmetics meet the requirements"; "dealer" includes a retailer; "Supplementary French language labelling requirements may apply to products sold in the Province of Quebec." - Industry Guide, link above
- s. 24(1) TEXT: "The label of a cosmetic that presents an avoidable hazard must include directions for safe use." Flammability symbol rules in ss. 25 to 26 are written for pressurized metal aerosol containers. - Cosmetic Regulations, link above

### Inferences
- MEANS: sourcing from a Canadian distributor that has notified and bilingually labelled the product keeps the shop a plain retailer. Importing directly makes the shop the importer with the CNF and label-compliance duties. Many Middle Eastern house bottles arrive with English/Arabic boxes only, which would fail s. 18 unless relabelled.
- MEANS: s. 15.3 puts a no-sale rule on "a person", so a retailer has a reason to ask each supplier for the notification confirmation.
- MEANS: for the listing, showing the INCI ingredient list (with allergens) on the product page mirrors the label and helps buyers; it is not stated as a website duty in the text I read.

### Gaps
- COULD NOT VERIFY from primary text whether a second importer of an identical, already-notified product must file its own CNF. The wording "Every manufacturer and importer" suggests yes. Settled by Health Canada's cosmetics program (cosmetics@hc-sc.gc.ca).
- Section 9.8 of the Industry Guide (Quebec) and the glass-atomizer flammability guidance were beyond the portion read.

## 5. Authenticity and sourcing: grey market, counterfeit, "inspired by" and Middle Eastern houses

### Takeaway
Selling genuine grey-market fragrance is generally lawful in Canada but unsettled at the edges; selling counterfeit is civil infringement and, with knowledge, a criminal offence. Lattafa, Armaf, Afnan and Rasasi are real brands selling under their own marks; the legal risk is in how a listing names the designer scent it resembles. Stripe lists counterfeit and "unauthorised sale of brand-name or designer products" as prohibited.

### Cited Findings
- Trademarks Act s. 51.01(1) TEXT: "Every person commits an offence who sells or offers for sale, or distributes on a commercial scale, any goods in association with a trademark, if that sale or distribution is or would be contrary to section 19 or 20 and the person knows that (a) the trademark is identical to, or cannot be distinguished in its essential aspects from, a trademark registered for such goods; and (b) the owner of that registered trademark has not consented". Penalty s. 51.01(6): on indictment "a fine of not more than $1,000,000 or to imprisonment for a term of not more than five years or to both". - [Trademarks Act, Justice Laws](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/T-13/page-8.html)
- s. 20(1) TEXT: infringement by any person who "(a) sells, distributes or advertises any goods or services in association with a confusing trademark or trade name". s. 20(1.01): infringement under (1)(b) "is presumed, unless the contrary is proven, if a person who is not entitled to use a registered trademark imports goods on a commercial scale that bear a trademark that is identical to, or cannot be distinguished in its essential aspects from, the trademark registered for such goods." s. 22(1): "No person shall use a trademark registered by another person in a manner that is likely to have the effect of depreciating the value of the goodwill attaching thereto." - [Trademarks Act ss. 19 to 22](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/acts/T-13/page-5.html)
- Grey goods: commentary says genuine parallel imports can typically be sold without infringing; in Mars Canada Inc. v. Bemco Cash & Carry Inc., 2018 ONCA 239, the court enforced a settlement barring a retailer from importing and noted the law is unsettled on whether a Canadian trademark holder can prevent grey marketing. - [Fasken, Can settlement agreements prohibit grey goods in Canada](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2018/04/can-settlement-agreements-prohibit-grey-goods-in-canada); [Gowling WLG, Exhaustion of patent and trademark rights](https://gowlingwlg.com/en/insights-resources/articles/2022/exhaustion-of-patent-and-trademark-rights)
- Dupes (non-Canadian commentary): scent formulas are generally not protected; names, logos, packaging and trade dress are; comparative claims draw suits when they mislead (Sol de Janeiro v. MCoBeauty, 2024, over "smell exactly like" claims). - [The Fashion Law, Squabbles Over Dupes Are Getting Their Day in Court](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/squabbles-over-dupes-are-getting-their-day-in-court/); [Mishcon de Reya, Perfume IP protection and the fight against dupes](https://www.mishcon.com/news/perfume-ip-protection-and-the-fight-against-dupes)
- Stripe prohibited list TEXT includes: "Counterfeit goods", "Illegally imported or exported products", "Unauthorised sale of brand-name or designer products or services", and separately "Toxic, flammable, combustible or radioactive materials". Tobacco products appear on its restricted list. - [Stripe, Prohibited and Restricted Businesses (en-ca)](https://stripe.com/en-ca/legal/restricted-businesses)

### Inferences
- MEANS: safest catalogue rule is a required supplier field and invoice reference per fragrance SKU, so authenticity can be shown to a brand owner, a processor or a customer.
- MEANS: writing a designer's registered name in a listing title or tag ("smells like X", "X dupe") is the exposure under ss. 20 and 22, not the scent itself. Describing notes and style without the other brand's mark avoids it. Canadian courts have applied s. 22 to a competitor's mark used in comparison on packaging (Clairol v. Thomas Supply, 1968; recalled from training, not fetched this session).
- MEANS: the new import presumption in s. 20(1.01) raises the stakes for direct importing of designer-branded stock; a legal read is needed on how it treats genuine goods.
- MEANS: Stripe's "flammable" line is likely aimed at hazardous materials businesses rather than cosmetics, but it is the kind of wording a risk review can cite.

### Gaps
- No Canadian case or Competition Bureau guidance on "inspired by" fragrance marketing was found. No reported dispute involving Lattafa, Armaf, Afnan or Rasasi in Canada was found (two searches).
- COULD NOT VERIFY: whether those houses have authorised Canadian distributors that file CNFs and supply bilingual labels. Settled by asking the wholesaler for the CNF confirmation.
- Shopify, PayPal, Moneris and Square policies were not read.

## 6. Returns and consumer rules: Ontario and Quebec

### Takeaway
Neither province's pages read give a general right to return an opened fragrance for change of mind; rights attach to non-delivery, missing disclosures and defects. Ontario: deliver within 30 days of the promised date, refund within 15 days of a valid cancellation. Quebec: contract copy within 15 days, cancellation rights at 7 or 30 days for merchant failures, refund within 15 days, chargeback as backstop.

### Cited Findings
- Ontario TEXT: "most agreements for a product or service that costs more than $50 must be in writing"; "A contract must clearly show the terms of your agreement with the business."; "For most contracts, the company has 15 days to return your money."; "you lose your right to cancel the contract if you accept the delivery after the 30 days" (page updated April 1, 2025). - [Ontario, Your rights when signing or cancelling a contract](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-when-signing-or-cancelling-contract)
- Ontario TEXT: a product "must be delivered within 30 days of the promised delivery date"; misrepresentation: "you can withdraw from the contract within 1 year." - [Ontario, Your rights under the Consumer Protection Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-under-consumer-protection-act)
- Ontario statute (search snippets of e-Laws): "A consumer may cancel an internet agreement within 30 days after the date the agreement is entered into, if the supplier does not comply with a requirement under section 39"; O. Reg. 17/05 has "Internet Agreements" ss. 31 (Prescribed amount), 32 (Disclosure of information), 33 (Copy of internet agreement), copy due "within 15 days after the consumer enters into the agreement". - [Consumer Protection Act, 2002](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/02c30); [O. Reg. 17/05](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/050017)
- Consumer Protection Act, 2023: e-Laws page states "THIS ACT IS NOT YET IN FORCE. It comes into force on a day to be named by proclamation" (as returned by search; a July 2026 secondary guide also said not in force). - [Consumer Protection Act, 2023](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23c23)
- Quebec TEXT (page updated 2 July 2026): "the merchant must provide you with a copy of the contract within 15 days of the purchase. You must be able to save and print it easily."; the contract "must indicate your name, your address and the date of the purchase" plus all pre-purchase information. - [OPC, Contents of the Contract](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/topic/purchase/online-purchase/contract/)
- Quebec cancellation grounds and limits TEXT: item not received "within 30 days following the date agreed upon in writing" or "within 30 days following the purchase date, if the delivery date was not specified" (cancel any time before receipt); no contract copy within 15 days (30 days after purchase); required information not given or "drowned out by a multitude of other information" (7 days after receiving the contract, the item, or the card statement); "The merchant did not clearly offer you the option of accepting or refusing the proposal, or to correct any errors". The OPC also tells consumers to first check "the merchant's exchange and refund policy". Refund due within 15 days of the notice, then chargeback (ss. 54.8 to 54.13 cited in the OPC sample notice). - [OPC, Conditions to Cancel an Online Purchase](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/topic/purchase/online-purchase/cancelling/condition/); [OPC sample cancellation notice](https://www.opc.gouv.qc.ca/en/consumer/topic/purchase/online-purchase/cancelling/sample-cancellation-notice)
- Quebec language: law-firm commentary says Charter of the French Language duties reach out-of-province sellers shipping to Quebec consumers, and the OQLF can order non-compliant goods not be sold online. - [Fasken, Doing ecommerce in Canada](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2025/05/doing-ecommerce-in-canada-heres-what-you-need-to-know); [Osler, Quebec further tightens language rules](https://www.osler.com/en/resources/regulations/2024/quebec-further-tightens-language-rules-for-commerce-and-business-what-you-need-to-know)
- Retailer practice: Le Labo Canada treats all creations as final sale; a Scarborough, Ontario shop limits returns to unopened items within 14 days; Maison Francis Kurkdjian accepts only factory-sealed items with cellophane intact and its return label is ground only; Sephora accepts gently used fragrance (window reported as 30 days in Canada since 2025, secondary source). - [Le Labo Canada, Cancellation rights](https://lelabofragrances.ca/faq/cancellation-rights.html); [My Royal Musk refund policy](https://www.myroyalmusk.com/policies/refund-policy); [Bargainmoose, Sephora Canada return policy](https://www.bargainmoose.ca/cosmetics/sephora-canada-return-policy-265465)

### Inferences
- MEANS: "sealed only, cellophane intact, opened bottles final sale except defects or wrong item" is a common and lawful-looking policy, provided it is disclosed before purchase and statutory cancellation rights (non-delivery, missing disclosure) are not excluded.
- MEANS: a returned cologne is still a dangerous good; the customer cannot lawfully mail it back at a Canada Post counter. The returns flow needs a carrier that accepts limited quantity or an in-store-only return rule for fragrance.
- MEANS: the checkout must show full terms before the buy button, let the buyer accept or correct, and email a savable order copy; that satisfies both provinces' core asks. The 30-day delivery clock interacts with ground-only shipping to remote areas, so promise dates for fragrance carts should be ground estimates.

## 7. Catalogue data for a cologne listing, and how fragrance retailers present it

### Takeaway
Beyond a normal product, a cologne SKU needs volume in mL, concentration, a hazard class driving shipping (flammable or not), ground-only and air-stage-blocked flags, supplier and compliance fields, and structured notes. Retailer product pages could not be read directly this session, so the presentation comparison is thin.

### Cited Findings
- Shipping-relevant facts the data must carry come from the rules above: Class 3 if flash point at or under 60 C ([TDG s. 2.18](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/FullText.html)); ground services only and no air stage ([Canada Post Customer Guide s. 6.2.2](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/customer-guide/section_6_the_abcs_of_shipping.pdf)); 30 kg box cap and mark ([TDG s. 1.17](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/page-4.html)).
- Label facts worth mirroring on the page: bilingual identity and net quantity, INCI list with allergens, contact information ([Cosmetic Regulations ss. 18, 20, 21.2, 21.4](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/C.R.C.,_c._869/FullText.html)).
- A Toronto brand's 50 mL eau de parfum page is marked final sale and gives a main note family plus sub notes rather than a top/heart/base pyramid; ships US and Canada only. - [Smythe, Eau de Parfum 50ml](https://shopsmythe.com/products/eau-de-parfum-50ml)
- A shipping-software vendor advises wording the restriction as "This item ships via ground transportation only" rather than "regulated flammable liquid". Vendor opinion. - [ShipAid, Why perfume and fragrance brands face shipping restrictions](https://www.shipaid.com/blogs/ecommerce-tips/why-perfume-and-fragrance-brands-face-shipping-restrictions-beauty-brands-never-have-to-plan-for)

### Inferences
- Suggested fields (my design, derived from the rules, not from a source): `volume_ml`; `concentration` (EDC, EDT, EDP, extrait/parfum, attar/oil, body mist); `alcohol_based` (bool); `flash_point_c` or `sds_on_file` (bool); `shipping_class` (standard, flammable_lq, lithium, non_shippable, pickup_only) as the single field the calculator reads; derived `ground_only` and `air_stage_blocked`; `un_number` (UN1266); `gender_positioning` (men, women, unisex); `notes_top`, `notes_heart`, `notes_base` (arrays, with a single `notes` fallback); `scent_family`; `brand`, `house_origin`; `supplier`, `supplier_invoice_ref`, `cnf_confirmed` (bool), `bilingual_label` (bool); `batch_code`, `received_date` (optional, back office only); `is_tester`, `is_boxed`, `sealed`; `returnable` (sealed only); `ingredients_inci`.
- Storefront: show size and concentration next to the name, notes as three short rows, a plain "Ships by ground only. Not available to some remote communities" line on the product page and again in cart when it changes the service list. Filters: brand, gender, concentration, size, scent family, price.
- Weight for the calculator must be packed weight (glass bottle plus box plus absorbent), not liquid volume; 100 mL of cologne typically ships at several times its liquid mass.

## 8. The "misc" category: avoiding a junk drawer, and which smoke-shop sundries are restricted for mail

### Takeaway
Shipping rules attach to the item, not the category, so every product needs its own shipping class regardless of where it sits in the menu. Several common sundries are harder than cologne: charcoal and butane cannot use the easy route, and tobacco cannot be mailed across provincial lines from a retailer to a consumer.

### Cited Findings
- Canada Post index, by class: Class 2 "Aerosol products", "Cigarette lighters", "Cylinders of compressed gas (oxygen, air, acetylene, butane, propane, carbon dioxide, and so on)"; Class 3 "Cigarette lighter fuel", "Essential oils (tea tree and eucalyptus oil)"; Class 4 "Charcoal", "Matches", "Fire starters"; Class 9 "Lithium batteries (with or without device)", "Strong magnets". All are non-mailable as dangerous goods unless under the contract/SfSB acceptance program. - [Canada Post, dangerous goods classes and index](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/non-mailable-matter/transportation-of-dangerous-goods-classes-and-index.page)
- TDG Schedule 1 limited quantity index, column 6(a), as read from the consolidated table (flattened text, confirm column alignment): UN1361 CARBON, animal or vegetable origin, Class 4.2: "0" (not eligible as limited quantity); UN1057 LIGHTERS or LIGHTER REFILLS containing flammable gas, 2.1: "0.125 L"; UN1011 BUTANE, 2.1: "0.125 L"; UN1944 MATCHES, SAFETY, 4.1: "5 kg"; UN1170 ETHANOL, 3, PG II: "1 L". - [TDG Regulations full text, Schedule 1](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2001-286/FullText.html)
- Lithium batteries, Canada Post table: installed in equipment, up to 4 cells or 2 batteries: no mark for one or two packages, accepted domestic air and surface; installed, more than that, or packed with equipment: lithium battery mark, not accepted by air (expanded program for contract and SfSB), accepted by surface but "Must not be shipped to remote Air Stage locations"; batteries on their own (including "Portable charging devices"): mark, surface only, no air stage. New 100 mm mark mandatory from January 1, 2027. - [Canada Post, Batteries](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/abcs-of-mailing/batteries.page)
- Vaping products TEXT: "non-mailable unless the: Lithium battery in the device meets the requirements ... for domestic ground/surface mailing of lithium batteries; Product is shipped using a ground service (for example, Expedited Parcel or Regular Parcel)" and "you can't ship vaping products to air stage offices." - [Non-mailable matter PDF s. 4.2](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/non-mailable-matter.pdf)
- Tobacco TEXT: "Tobacco products are non-mailable from one province or territory to another unless the: Shipment is between manufacturers or retailers; Shipment is between two consumers; Mailer is otherwise expressly permitted by federal law"; "Proof of Age is required for all tobacco product shipments unless the shipment is between" those parties. - same, s. 4.2
- Knives: the index note says it "doesn't necessarily include items designated as non-mailable matter under our regulations. For example, knives that have been improperly prepared for mailing." Sharp items: rigid corrugated box, blades wrapped "100 mm beyond the tip", mark "HANDLE WITH CARE". - [Non-mailable matter PDF s. 6.2](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/doc/en/support/non-mailable-matter.pdf); [Examples of how to package and wrap items](https://www.canadapost-postescanada.ca/cpc/en/support/articles/abcs-of-mailing/examples-of-how-to-package-and-wrap-items.page)
- Replica or inert munitions (grenade-shaped novelties) "aren't allowed in the mail under any circumstances." - Non-mailable matter PDF s. 5.2, link above
- Taxonomy: Baymard's public research names over-categorisation and redundant or overlapping categories as the main category faults; nothing public was found specifically on "Other"/"Misc" sections. - [Baymard, Homepage and Category usability](https://baymard.com/research/homepage-and-category-usability)

### Inferences
- MEANS: hookah charcoal is the item to settle first, since the shop already sells hookah goods: Canada Post's own index names charcoal, and UN1361 has a limited quantity index of 0. Whether a given coconut-shell hookah charcoal is actually classed as UN1361 depends on a self-heating test result the manufacturer holds; without that document it should be treated as not shippable by Canada Post.
- MEANS: empty, never-filled lighters and torches are likely not dangerous goods, filled ones and butane canisters are (Class 2.1, 125 mL limited quantity cap per container, which rules out standard 300 mL butane cans). Not confirmed with Canada Post.
- Suggested handling (my design): name the section by what is in it once there are enough items ("Lighters and torches", "Gifts and lifestyle", "Home fragrance", "Storage and cases") and keep "More" as a menu label only; store one real `category` plus free `tags`; make `shipping_class` a required per-item field with values such as standard, flammable_lq, lithium_surface, pickup_only, age_restricted; add boolean `age_restricted` and `interprovincial_blocked`; the back-office form refuses to save an item in the catch-all without a shipping class; review the catch-all when it passes a set count and promote clusters into named categories.
- Cart rule: the cart's allowed services are the intersection of every item's allowed services; pickup_only items remove shipping entirely or split the order.
